For the Observer

LitRPGSim is a browser game (React/TypeScript). It began April 15, 2021 as a satirical, procedurally generated LitRPG made entirely of menus. Zampanio first appears in the commit log on July 28, 2021, and from then on it becomes ZampanioSim: a game you are meant to hack, full of hidden modes. In late 2021 the repository itself became a labyrinth. Branches are rooms, named by compass direction.

Preservation warning: most of the story lives in metadata: commit messages, author emails, dates and branch names. GitHub’s “Download ZIP” keeps one branch’s files and none of that. Keep a full mirror (git clone --mirror, about 930 MB). EastBackup contains a gource script that renders the whole history as a video, which would make a good second format.

The compass key is already in master’s code. To the NORTH is ThisIsNotAGame, full of “wistful Might-Have-Beens.” To the SOUTH is JustTruth, with “no more masks.” To the EAST is ThisIsAGame, “a place of lies and madness.” HydrationSim restates it: East is raw chaos, North is more of the same, South is the destruction of illusions. There is no West.

The hacked master log (read it bottom-up)

On Dec 30, 2021, 217 commit messages were rewritten in under an hour. The original dates were kept, so each line of the story sits on the day the real work happened.

  1. Discovery. JR is minding their own business, keeping SBURBSim’s survival rate at “a VERY respectable 70%,” when they find a creepypasta about a game that obviously doesn’t exist. It’s freeing: a fandom “all in on the joke.”

  2. The lore. They catalogue the claims: released April 1, 1972 in Italy, cracks that vanish, a locked Discord with a dead owner, uncanny personalization, custom titles, absurd bugs. “i can feel my obsessive throes rising.”

  3. Permission. Zampanio is “gloriously, freeingly not real,” so nothing JR does to it has consequences. JR stops touching SBURBSim and makes ZampanioSim.

  4. Testimony. Then JR actually plays it, hidden inside a PowerPoint. They spawn as a “Waste of Lies.” Friends generated from JR’s own name die horribly while the game blames and rewards them. JR starts a Discord so the records won’t vanish again.

  5. Panic. One-word commits: “if its REAL,” “did i fuck up,” “my operating system.” Mid-panic, the author email switches to justifiedRecursion. That switch is in the original commits too, so the possession was performed live months before the rewrite.

  6. The new voice. Cheerful and full of exclamation points. It was all according to plan. It had always already happened. “Refs: #22917 is part of it now. I am part of it now!!!” Maybe JR made the game real by “wasting” it (“Waste” is SBURBSim’s hacker class).

  7. Takeover. “i have the book now. MY book.” It has taken the names of the Discord members, lures new ones in with a real invite link, and hates how “linear” things are: “MUCH better to have it spiralling out in all directions!!!”

  8. Coda. Jan 25, 2022, back as jadedResearcher: “it only makes sense for things to never end.” That commit begins recording the player’s route in local storage: 0 for the menu, 1 for ThisIsAGame, 2 for JustTruth, and 13 on leaving, “not a real direction.”

Emotional beats and themes

The core arc is freedom curdling into obsession, then possession. East gives obsession a human cost. Wodin stops sleeping and quits his job to hunt a killer while his friend begs, “i just want my friend back.” After the coffin, the friend keeps messaging someone who can’t answer (“i’m not going to forget you”), and then falls into the same hole himself.

Erasure recurs everywhere: the Octome, the Tres Bottle, one-way tickets, “Forget Everything?” So does loneliness turned into collaboration. Commits credit community members by title (Herald, Weaver, Theorist, Watcher). In South, Truth says JR turns glitches into lore as “a symptom of how Lonely they are.” Brokenness is treated as reverence: “Is using buggy code an act of reverence?”

On Dec 28, 2021 a meme was added reading “there is a point where we needed to stop… but let’s keep going.” The latest branch resolves it: “If you stop finding things… it’s time to start leaving things for those who come after.”

Assumptions and alternative hypotheses

  • “Each branch is a different take” holds for about 14 of the 21 (95% confident). The rest are twins, snapshots or one-commit experiments.

    95%

  • Master isn’t the only hacked history. Octome and TresBottle were rewritten first, and the North family inherits master’s fiction (95%).

    95%

  • The compass explains the branch names (85%). The alternative is that the names were practical deploy URLs, mythologized afterward (15%).

    85%

  • The one censored line in master’s story (“Refs: #22917 is part of it now”) is Octome bleed-through (70%). If so, the hidden name is most likely Truth (50%), then Nam (25%), then the Minotaur (15%).

    70%

  • Octome’s “reverse mode” commits. It might be the artifact’s mirror effect, where hidden information becomes easy to find by comparing branches (50%). Or it’s just deeper corruption, since it redacts more (50%).

    50%

  • The identity switch was deliberate kayfabe (65%). It could instead be a practical git-config change later folded into the lore (35%); an earlier email change was plainly practical (“new dev env, who this”).

    65%
